On the thirtieth of {{frostfall}}, 171 of the {{era-fourth}}, a {{thalmor}} envoy brought a covered cart into the {{imperial-city}} and laid his demands before Emperor {{titus-mede-ii}}: a vast tribute, the disbanding of the {{blades}}, a ban on the worship of {{talos}}, and the cession of much of {{hammerfell}} to the {{aldmeri-dominion}}.

{{titus-mede-ii|Titus Mede II}} had been emperor for three years. His generals warned him that the legions were weak, but he refused. The envoy then overturned the cart. It held more than a hundred heads. Some in the hall recognized them as {{blades}} agents who had been sent into the elves' lands.

Within days the {{aldmeri-dominion|Dominion}} attacked. One army crossed western {{cyrodiil}} into {{hammerfell}}. The other, led by {{naarifin}}, marched north from {{elsweyr}} and took city after city. By the end of the next year {{naarifin}} reached the capital, and in 174 the {{imperial-city}} fell. {{titus-mede-ii|Titus Mede}} broke out to the north with his main army, leaving one legion behind; it died to the last man. The palace was burned and the {{white-gold-tower}} was sacked.

That winter the {{thalmor}} believed the war was won and sent envoys to talk terms. Meanwhile the emperor gathered his forces. In spring he returned with legions from {{hammerfell}} and {{skyrim}}, surrounded the {{imperial-city}}, and destroyed the main elven army in {{cyrodiil}} at the {{battle-of-the-red-ring}}. {{naarifin}} was captured and hung from the {{white-gold-tower|tower}} for thirty-three days.

The Empire had won, but its legions were exhausted. That same year {{titus-mede-ii|Titus}} signed the {{white-gold-concordat}}, accepting most of the demands he had refused, including the ban on {{talos}}. {{hammerfell}} rejected the treaty; to keep the peace, the emperor gave up the province, and the {{redguards}} fought on alone. In {{skyrim}}, many {{nords}} went on worshipping {{talos}} in private.

## Notes

- **Whose account.** The story follows *The Great War*, a soldier's history pieced together from captured papers, prisoners and survivors; its author, a legate wounded in the assault on the capital, admits that some gaps are filled by educated guesswork. The {{thalmor}} call the conflict the "First War Against the Empire".
- **Who won the day.** Imperial histories give the victory to {{titus-mede-ii|Titus}} himself, who is said to have captured {{naarifin}} in person. Another account claims the emperor, struck down by an assassin, sent a nameless champion, the {{forgotten-hero}}, to lead the assault in his place and in his guise, and that this champion defeated {{naarifin}} while the emperor kept the glory.
- **The general's end.** Thirty-three days on the {{white-gold-tower|tower}} is the accepted version; one source says that on the thirty-fourth day a winged {{daedra|daedra}} carried him away.
