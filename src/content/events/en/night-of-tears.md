Late in the {{era-merethic}}, {{ysgramor}} led a band of settlers south across the sea from {{atmora}} and founded {{saarthal}} on the coast of {{skyrim}}. It was the first city of men in that land.

The land was ruled by the {{falmer}}. At first the two peoples lived side by side in peace.

While digging the city's foundations, the settlers found something deep underground. The men who uncovered it could not say what it was; they only felt uneasy, and none would go near it again. The settlers buried it once more and swore to keep it secret.

The secret did not hold. Once word reached the elves, dealings between the two peoples dwindled, and quarrels along the border grew.

Then one night the {{falmer}} fell on {{saarthal}}. They burned the city and killed its people. Only three survived: {{ysgramor}} and his two sons, {{yngol}} and {{ylgar}}, who reached the last ship still whole and escaped.

At sea, {{ysgramor}} wept as he watched the city burn, and his tears turned to ebony. {{yngol}}, the finest smith of his people, gathered them and forged a great axe overnight. {{ysgramor}} named it {{wuuthrad}}, which in the tongue of {{atmora}} means the Storm's Tears.

The ship sailed for {{atmora}}.

No one at the time left any record of what lay beneath the city. Only in the {{era-fourth}}, when mages of the {{college-of-winterhold}} excavated the ruins, was it identified as the {{eye-of-magnus}}.

## Notes

- **Why the elves struck.** This telling follows the explanation tied to what lay under the city, argued in the inquiry *Night of Tears*. The {{college-of-winterhold}} later found the {{eye-of-magnus|Eye}} under the ruins, which shows that something did lie beneath the city, but not that the elves attacked for it. Elven writers instead blame {{ysgramor}} for unspecified "provocations and blasphemies"; human scholars answer that the {{falmer}} simply feared a young, vigorous people who might one day outgrow them.
- **Survivors.** Tradition counts only {{ysgramor}} and his sons. Yet one warrior who later sailed with the {{five-hundred-companions}} on {{ylgar}}'s ship claimed that he, too, had lived through the night.
- **Dates.** No year survives. One account sets {{saarthal}}'s founding in the middle of the {{era-merethic}}, while the tale of {{ysgramor}}'s own voyage places the migration near its end; the attack itself falls late in the era.
- **The axe.** The ebony tears and the forging of {{wuuthrad}} come from {{nords|Nord}} legend; in the tongue of {{atmora}} the name means "Storm's Tears".
