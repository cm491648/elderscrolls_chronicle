The sentence on {{lorkhan}} was carried out by {{trinimac}}, the champion of {{auriel}} and the greatest of his knights. In front of {{lorkhan}}'s followers, {{trinimac}} struck him down and reached into him, with more than hands, and tore out his heart.

{{lorkhan}} was undone. His followers carried his body away.

The heart did not die. When {{trinimac}} and {{auriel}} tried to destroy it, it mocked them. It was the heart of the world now, it said, for the one had been made to satisfy the other; as long as the world lasted, the heart would too. {{auriel}} fastened it to an arrow and shot it far out over the sea, where no one would ever find it. Where it fell, the earth rose up and a mountain of fire was born.

What was left of {{lorkhan}} went on without its heart, wandering the world he had set in motion. The gods who had judged him withdrew; he alone remained among mortals.

In later ages the mountain of fire was called {{red-mountain}}, and the heart within it the {{heart-of-lorkhan}}.

## Notes

- **Other endings.** Some {{altmer}} say he was tried and slain atop the {{adamantine-tower}}, and the heart was shot from there. Other cultures hold that he gave up his heart of his own will.
- **The north.** The {{nords}} call him {{shor}}. They say elven giants killed their chief god and carried his heart as a standard to frighten men, and that before he faded from the world he built {{sovngarde}}, where the honored dead go.
- **The pieces.** Some believe his divine being was scattered across {{tamriel}}, and that ebony is his blood. To the {{khajiit}}, the third, dark moon is his corpse.
- **Source.** The knight, the talking heart and the arrow come from the {{altmer}} myth "The Heart of the World".
