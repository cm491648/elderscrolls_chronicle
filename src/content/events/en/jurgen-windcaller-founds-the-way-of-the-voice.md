In the {{era-first}} the {{nords}} made war with the Voice. Its masters were called {{tongues}}, and a Shout from one of them could break a city gate; the strongest went about gagged. The war chiefs who carried the {{nords}} east and built their first empire were {{tongues}}, and {{jurgen-windcaller}} was among the mightiest of them.

In 416 the {{chimer}} and the {{dwemer}} beat the {{nords}} at {{red-mountain}} and drove them out of {{resdayn}}. The {{tongues}} came home humbled. {{jurgen-windcaller|Jurgen}} turned his thoughts to the defeat and spent seven years in meditation, asking how the Voice could have failed them.

He came to believe that the {{nords}} had grown proud of the Voice, as the dragons had before them, and that the gods had punished them for turning it to conquest. The Voice, he taught, was a gift to be used for the glory of the gods, not the glory of men, and as a road to wisdom rather than to power. This teaching became the {{way-of-the-voice}}.

He took it back to the {{tongues}}, and they would not accept it. Seventeen of them came against him at once. {{jurgen-windcaller|Jurgen}} did not shout back. For three days he swallowed their Shouts in silence, until they had nothing left. Then they owned him their master and followed him up the {{throat-of-the-world}}, where he made his home on the high slopes. His followers became the {{greybeards}}, the silent masters of {{high-hrothgar}}. The {{nords}} remembered him as {{jurgen-windcaller|Jurgen the Calm}}.

When he died, he was laid to rest in {{ustengrav}}, an ancient tomb in the marshes of the north, and his horn was buried with him.

## Notes

- **The defeat.** No source gives the year, but the tablets of {{high-hrothgar}} tell of the defeat at {{red-mountain}} right after the founding of the empire, which points to 416.
- **Another reading.** Others place it at the {{battle-of-red-mountain}} around 700, where the army of the {{nords}} was destroyed. A guide to the Empire also counts {{hoag-merkiller}}, who died in 482, among the warrior {{tongues}} before {{jurgen-windcaller|Jurgen}}.
- **The years.** The tablets give seven years of meditation; the {{greybeards}} themselves speak only of many years.
- **Silence.** The {{greybeards}} seldom speak, lest their voices shake the land. Long afterward they broke their silence to proclaim the destiny of {{tiber-septim}}.
