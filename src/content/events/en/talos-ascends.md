{{tiber-septim}} reigned for eighty-one years, and many came to count him the greatest emperor who ever lived. Late in his reign, the holy site of {{sancre-tor}}, where the emperors of old lay buried, was corrupted by an evil no one could name. The four greatest {{blades}} of the day went to learn what had happened and never came back, and the Grandmaster of the {{blades}} had the ruins sealed.

In {{era-third|3E}} 38 {{tiber-septim}} died at the age of 108. There had been talk that his court mages had found ways to lengthen his life, or that he had been granted immortality, but he died all the same. On his deathbed he passed the throne to his heir, {{pelagius-i}}. Some of the treasures linked to him disappeared after his death.

His worshippers came to believe that he had risen to {{aetherius}} and taken his place at the right hand of {{akatosh}}, so that the {{eight-divines}} became nine. Under the name {{talos}}, Stormcrown, he was honoured as the patron of warriors, generals and adventurers.

Centuries later the {{aldmeri-dominion}}, holding that no man could become a god, forced the Empire to ban his worship.

## Notes

- **How he died.** Most accounts say old age; some claim he never recovered from a wound taken at {{sancre-tor}}.
- **How he rose.** He is said to have walked one or more of the Walking Ways, the paths by which a mortal may become a god.
- **The corruption.** It was later learned to be the work of the {{underking}}, once {{tiber-septim}}'s own battlemage, who cursed the four {{blades}} to guard the ruins as undead.
- **The date.** One source puts the corruption in {{era-third|3E}} 36; another, in the thirty-sixth year of his reign.
- **A rumour.** It was whispered that he never died at all but became the {{underking}}; the story is generally held to be false.
