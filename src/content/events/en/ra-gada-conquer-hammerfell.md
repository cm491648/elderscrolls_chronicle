The first warriors to cross from {{yokuda}} were the {{sword-singers}} who had sailed east with {{frandar-hunding}}. On the far shore they found a desert ruled by giant {{goblins}}, whose strongholds stood all over the sands. {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}} would not share the land with them and went to war. He was killed in one of the early battles. His son {{divad-hunding}} took command and fought on until the {{alikr-desert}} was theirs.

Behind them, {{yokuda}} sank into the sea. More ships came, carrying warriors and the people they guarded. The {{na-totambu}}, the old ruling class, and most of the refugees were put ashore on the island of {{herne}}. The warriors sailed on, and in {{era-first|1E}} 808 they landed on the mainland at {{hegathe}}, on the south-west coast.

From there they spread along the southern and western shores and pushed inland. One fleet, under a grandee named {{yaghoub}}, rounded the cape into the {{iliac-bay}} and founded {{sentinel}} on the northern coast. {{goblins}}, {{orcs}}, {{nedes}} and {{bretons}} fell before them, and so did the elves who held old ruins in the hills. After only a few great battles the whole province was theirs. The beastfolk who survived fled north to join the {{orcs}} at {{orsinium}}.

The newcomers were known as the {{ra-gada}}, the Warrior Waves. In the mouths of other peoples the name became {{redguards|Redguard}}, and in time it came to mean the whole people, not only the warriors. The land they had taken became {{hammerfell}}.

## Notes

- **Waves.** The crossing came in four waves over about twenty-eight years. The first, with the Hundings, sailed around {{era-first|1E}} 780, before {{yokuda}} sank; the second came around the time of the sinking.
- **Father and son.** {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}} is often credited with the victory over the {{goblins}}, although he died early in that war; the deeds of father and son are sometimes told under the one name of Hunding.
- **The name.** Another telling says the name was born in {{yokuda}} itself: after the last battle against its emperor, the people saw their guardians as red with blood.
- **Later.** Once the warriors held the land, the old nobility followed and took up its rule again. The divide between the nobles' heirs and the warriors' heirs lasted into later eras.
