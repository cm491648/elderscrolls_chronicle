In the fifth century of the {{era-first}}, two powers stood face to face in the west. From {{cyrodiil}}, the {{alessian-order}} pushed its empire westward, meaning to rid {{tamriel}} of elves and of magic. In {{high-rock}}, the elven {{direnni}} clan held most of the land and had given refuge to the last {{ayleid}} fleeing the Order.

In {{era-first|1E}} 478 a lord of {{cyrodiil}} beat the Alessian emperor in battle. Encouraged, the {{direnni}} outlawed the Order's doctrines in {{high-rock}}. The Order answered with war. {{aiden-direnni}}, who led the clan's forces, signed a charter that gave the {{bretons}} rights in return for service, and took them into his army.

In 482 a vast Alessian army, three times the size of his, met him on the {{glenumbra}} moors. The enchantress {{raven-direnni}} worked a magic no one had seen before: it lit the sky, made the rocks give way, and turned the ground under her own soldiers into blessed ground. {{direnni}} mages rode into battle on horses of conjured fire. {{bretons|Breton}} knights fought in the elven ranks, beside the last {{ayleid}} king. Even the High King of {{skyrim}} came. He shared the Order's hatred of elves, but the Alessians had killed the king before him, and he fell on the moors.

The {{direnni}} won, at great cost. The {{alessian-order}} was driven out of {{high-rock}}.

## Notes

- **Afterward.** The Order never tried to take {{high-rock}} again. The clan had lost so much that within twenty years the {{bretons}} had taken most of the province from it.
- **The Order.** The defeat was long counted among the Order's worst follies, and a cause of its decline.
- **The High King.** He was {{hoag-merkiller}}; not every account agrees that he died in the battle. His successor brought back the old {{nords|Nordic}} gods and burned the priests of the new faith.
- **Memory.** {{bretons}} sang of the battle for centuries and kept a holiday for it, the Day of Release. Travelers on the moors long afterward told of hearing the sound of fighting.
- **Sources.** Few details of the fighting were kept. Some records spell the name Glenumbria.
- **Date.** The memoir of the last {{ayleid}} king gives {{era-first|1E}} 482; a Nordic record puts the start of the next High King's reign in 480, which would set the battle two years earlier.
