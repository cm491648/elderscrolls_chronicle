When {{vivec}} vanished at the end of the {{era-third}}, the god who had held {{baar-dau}} in the air for thousands of years was gone. The great rock still hung over {{vivec-city}}, the city that bore his name on the southern shore of {{vvardenfell}}.

In 5 of the {{era-fourth}} it fell. A trader on the road south of the city noticed every sound around him stop and his pack animal grow frantic. Then a great blast tore through the heart of {{vivec-city}}, and he saw its stone cantons fall apart. A few minutes later, on the 3rd of {{suns-dawn}}, {{red-mountain}} erupted, throwing a huge cloud of fire into the sky.

A wall of flame swept across the island. In the west, {{gnisis}} was wiped away. Across the water on {{solstheim}}, the walls of {{fort-frostmoth}} crumbled. On the mainland, the ground around {{tear}} turned to mush: the swamp swallowed half the city, and its southern wall collapsed into the water.

{{mournhold}}, the capital, was spared. Many there had kin on {{vvardenfell}}, and every day brought word of ruin from its towns. Some said the {{tribunal}} had protected the city; others blamed the {{tribunal}} for everything, and the quarrels came to blows. About a month after the eruption, a councillor of {{house-redoran}} living in {{mournhold}} took charge and sent soldiers, supplies and able-bodied {{dunmer}} to the stricken settlements.

{{dunmer|Dunmer}} who lived abroad came home to help. For years afterwards refugees fled the province, many of them to {{skyrim}} and to {{solstheim}}.

## Notes

- **The sources.** Most of what is known comes from the survivors' accounts gathered in *The Red Year* and from the last letter of a soldier trapped in {{fort-frostmoth}}.
- **The witness.** The trader on the road only learned much later that the rock had struck the city.
- **The god.** What became of {{vivec}} is disputed: some say he died, others that he simply disappeared.
