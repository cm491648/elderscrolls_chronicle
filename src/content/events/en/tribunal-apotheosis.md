Some years after the {{battle-of-red-mountain}}, {{almalexia}}, {{vivec}} and {{sotha-sil}} became living gods. Their people, whose skin had turned the grey of ash and whose eyes had turned red, were afraid.

When {{azura}} rebuked the three, {{sotha-sil}} answered her. Her time as a god was over, he said; the new gods would be wise and would care for their people. Then he spoke to the frightened {{chimer}}. What had happened to them was no curse but a sign. They were a new people, the {{dunmer}}, who would no longer toil under distant gods but would deal face to face with gods of their own. The people took heart.

The three chose their own faces. {{sotha-sil}} kept the grey skin of his people. {{almalexia}} returned to the gold of the {{chimer}}. {{vivec}} took half of each.

Together they were called {{almsivi}}, and the faith that gathered around them became the {{tribunal-temple}}. {{almalexia}}, the Healing Mother, ruled from {{mournhold}} and cared for the poor and the sick. {{vivec}}, the warrior-poet, watched over {{vvardenfell}} and {{red-mountain}}. {{sotha-sil}} withdrew into study and building. The old worship of {{boethiah}}, {{azura}} and {{mephala}} gave way, and {{veloth}} was honored as a saint.

The Temple taught that the three had won their divinity through discipline, virtue and wisdom. Of the {{heart-of-lorkhan}}, it said nothing.

## Notes

- **The way to godhood.** {{sotha-sil}} had studied {{kagrenacs-tools}} and learned how to use them on the {{heart-of-lorkhan}}. He brought the other two a vision of a just world with the three as its guides, and together they went to the Heart's chamber under {{red-mountain}}.
- **Other accounts.** {{vivec}}'s own sermons speak of the three as divine long before {{red-mountain}}. They also say the change of color was {{boethiah}}'s wish, brought about by {{vivec}}'s grief.
- **Afterward.** Their power waned in the {{era-third}} in the long struggle against {{dagoth-ur}}. After the {{nerevarine}} ended it, the Temple returned to the three good {{daedra}} and kept the three only as saints.
- **Date.** About {{era-first|1E}} 700; the sources say only that it was a few years after the battle.
