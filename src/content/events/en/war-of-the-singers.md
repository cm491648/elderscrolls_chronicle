After the death of its ruler, the empire of {{yokuda}} fell into civil war, and from the chaos a man named {{hira}} took the throne. To hold the empire in his own hands, he set out to destroy the {{sword-singers}}, the masters of the blade who stood high among the people.

The singers were few and scattered, and he struck first at those best known. He sent a hundred of his guards for {{divad-hunding}}, whose songs were known across the land. They took him at his home, and one of them struck down his old mother. {{divad-hunding|Divad}} broke free, killed four of the guards with his own chains and escaped. From then on he appeared wherever the emperor's plans went wrong.

{{divad-hunding|Divad}}'s father, {{frandar-hunding}}, the greatest swordsman of the age, had withdrawn to a cave on {{mount-hattu}} to write down what he knew of the sword. He wanted to bargain with {{hira}} and spare the lives a war would cost. Father and son argued for five hours. When they came out, {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}} had agreed to lead.

The singers who had survived gathered at the foot of the mountain. {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}} laid out seven battles, each drawing the emperor's army deeper into the wilderness and teaching his own people more of his way. The first six ended with neither side winning. The seventh was fought at the foot of {{mount-hattu}} in {{era-first|1E}} 780, against an army thirty times their number. The singers won. Fewer than twenty thousand of them lived; more than three hundred thousand of the emperor's soldiers died.

The people did not thank them. With no welcome left at home, {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}} and his army went to the port of {{arch}}, took ship and sailed east across the sea.

## Notes

- **The sword.** The singers fought with the {{shehai}}, a blade formed from the swordsman's own spirit. {{frandar-hunding|Frandar}}'s writings on the way of the sword became the *Book of Circles*.
- **Later.** The warriors who sailed with him were the first wave of the {{ra-gada}}, and their landing in the new country is told in a separate entry.
