In {{era-third|3E}} 249 a sorcerer named {{camoran-usurper|Haymon}}, adviser to {{camoran-kaltos}}, a claimant to the old throne of the {{camoran-dynasty}}, began a war to win {{valenwood}}. His army of mercenaries, {{daedra}} and the undead took nearly all of the province within two years. With the throne won, he turned on the west of {{cyrodiil}} and sacked {{kvatch}}. {{camoran-kaltos|Kaltos}}, appalled at the slaughter of townsfolk, begged him to come home. Instead the sorcerer turned on his lord and took the throne for himself, and {{camoran-kaltos|Kaltos}} fled. From then on he was the {{camoran-usurper}}.

Emperor {{cephorus-ii}}, fighting a war over his own succession, could do little. The mercenaries he sent into {{hammerfell}} were bribed, raised as undead or slaughtered. Over thirteen years the {{camoran-usurper|Usurper}} conquered most of the province.

In 266 he came to {{high-rock}}. The many small kingdoms of the {{iliac-bay}} had no leader, little love for the Empire, and some made secret pacts with him. But word of his cruelty to captives spread, and early in 267 {{othrok|Baron Othrok}} of {{dwynnen}} brought three neighbouring kingdoms into an alliance. Joined by {{camoran-kaltos|Kaltos}}, they gathered the greatest fleet ever seen and met the {{camoran-usurper|Usurper}}'s forces on land around {{dwynnen}} and at sea in the bay. The weather turned against him, his army was destroyed, and he fell in the battle.

The West had saved itself without the Emperor, and the Empire's standing there sank further.

## Notes

- **His son.** One account says his son {{mankar-camoran}}, who later founded the cult behind the {{oblivion-crisis}}, was born during the battle; other writings are hard to square with that date.
- **Origins.** Where he came from is unknown. A persistent legend makes him the son of {{molag-bal}} and a {{bretons|Breton}} woman.
- **The weather.** The storm that turned against him was later counted as divine intervention.
