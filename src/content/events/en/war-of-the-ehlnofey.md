Before there was a world, the brothers {{anu}} and {{padomay}} wandered the void. Creation was born, and life grew on twelve worlds. After long ages {{padomay}} came back, hated what he saw, and smashed the twelve worlds apart. {{anu}} fought and beat him, then gathered the broken pieces into a single world, {{nirn}}.

{{nirn}} was one great landmass with inland seas and no oceans. Only two peoples had come through the ruin of the twelve worlds: the {{hist}}, the great trees of the marshes, and the {{ehlnofey}}.

The {{ehlnofey}} did not arrive together. One group landed on a large piece of their old world that had held together. They walled it off from the chaos outside and went on living as before. This hidden land was {{old-ehlnofey}}. The rest were scattered across the wreckage, and they spent many years searching for one another.

At last these Wanderers found {{old-ehlnofey}}. They had expected to be welcomed home. Its people instead looked on them as degenerate and fallen, and in the end war broke out across the whole of {{nirn}}. The people of {{old-ehlnofey}} still had their ancient power and learning. The Wanderers had greater numbers, and their long struggle had made them hard.

The war reshaped the world. New oceans drowned much of the land and left four continents: {{tamriel}}, {{atmora}}, {{akavir}} and {{yokuda}}. The ruins of {{old-ehlnofey}} became {{tamriel}}, and over the years its people became the elves. The Wanderers, split among the other three continents, became the races of men. On {{atmora}} they were the {{nords}}, on {{yokuda}} the {{redguards}}, and on {{akavir}} a people of their own. The {{hist}} took no side, but the war passed over their land and sank most of it. One corner survived as {{black-marsh}}.

## Notes

- **Whose telling.** The story follows the *Anuad*, an old creation myth best known in a short retelling, *The Annotated Anuad*.
- **The elven view.** {{altmer}} myth treats the {{ehlnofey}} as divine spirits who chose to stay in the new world, and tells instead of a war between {{auriel}} and {{lorkhan}} and their followers. The two stories mirror each other closely and may record the same conflict from different points of view.
- **Omitted.** The *Anuad* adds that the interplay of {{anu}} and {{padomay}} produced a goddess who bore creation and died of the wounds {{padomay}} gave her.
