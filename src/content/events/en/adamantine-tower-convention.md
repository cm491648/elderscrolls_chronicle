After {{magnus}} left, the world was half made, and the spirits who remained had to decide what to do with it. {{auriel}} called them together. Where the bones of the new earth met, he set down his silver vessel, and it became a spike of metal driven into the ground: the {{adamantine-tower}}. The spirits knew that whatever they agreed upon there would hold for all time.

They settled three things. First, the world. Some of them gave themselves wholly to it and became its bones, the laws by which it would run from then on. Others, the {{ehlnofey}}, chose to live upon the world rather than become part of it. Their children, a little weaker in each generation, became the first mortals.

Second, {{lorkhan}}, whose scheme had cost them so dearly. They judged him, and passed sentence.

Third, themselves. So long as they stayed, the young world could not hold its shape. They agreed to withdraw from its affairs, and with them went the boundless magic of the beginning. Time began to run in a single line, and the {{era-dawn}} came to an end.

The gods abandoned the tower and left its secrets behind. Mortals later found it on the island of {{balfiera}} in the {{iliac-bay}}, the oldest structure known in {{tamriel}}.

## Notes

- **Another view.** The {{mythic-dawn}} taught the opposite: the world was {{lorkhan}}'s own realm, and at the tower the gods stole it from him and cut mortals off from their divine spark, so that they would look to the gods as their only way out.
- **Date.** A magical divination places the tower's building around {{era-merethic|ME}} 2500, while the myth sets the Convention in the {{era-dawn}}, for which no dates can be fixed.
- **Later.** In the {{era-merethic}} the elves of the {{direnni}} clan took the tower and ruled from it. The {{ayleid}} built {{white-gold-tower}} in imitation of it.
