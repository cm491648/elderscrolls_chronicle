In the {{era-first}}, the {{alessian-order}} ruled the empire of {{cyrodiil}} as a strict faith of one god, hostile to everything elven. Its most zealous sect was the {{marukhati-selective}}. The elves worshipped the time god as {{auriel}}. The {{marukhati-selective|Selective}} believed that {{akatosh}}, the time god of men, had been tainted with this elven {{auriel}}, and they set out to cut the elven part away. Their masters worked a great rite upon the god himself.

In 1200 of the {{era-first}}, time broke.

What people lived through afterward could not be set down as one history. When the {{elder-council}} later gathered accounts from every province, no two agreed. The souls of past emperors, speaking through the {{amulet-of-kings}}, remembered {{cyrodiil}} as an empire spread across the stars; other {{cyrodiil|Cyrodiilic}} accounts said it had become an egg. The {{dunmer}} wrote of borders that would not stay in place. Every account mentioned one thing: eight stars fell from the sky, one after another, and people counted their days by them.

When time ran straight again, the {{elder-council}} reckoned that the break had lasted one thousand and eight years and ended in 2208. It was named the {{dragon-break}}. About a century later the {{war-of-righteousness}} destroyed the {{alessian-order}}, and its records were lost with it.

## Notes

- **Doubts.** The later scholar {{fal-droon}} argued that time never broke: an early encyclopedia had misread the calendar of the {{alessian-order|Alessian}} priesthood, and the {{alessian-order|Order}} had really lasted about 150 years. Writings from the {{era-second}} already describe the {{dragon-break}}, which tells against him. Copies of his book were circulating by {{era-third|3E}} 427, yet it mentions events that had not yet happened.
- **The rite.** Later sources link the break to the misuse of a powerful staff by the {{marukhati-selective|Selective}}. Whether the {{marukhati-selective|Selective}} achieved what they wanted is unknown.
