In the {{era-first}}, {{the-reach}} was free, but it was not at peace. Ten kings of the {{reachmen}} ruled ten small kingdoms and made war on one another. A boy was born in the hills there, and his people named him {{red-eagle|Faolan}}, which in their tongue means {{red-eagle|Red Eagle}}, for the cry of the bird that greeted his birth and the red flowers on the autumn slopes. The augurs said he would be a warrior without equal and would unite the ten kingdoms under his name.

Then {{hestra}}, the Empress of the South, came north with her legions. Some of the kings she beat in the field; the rest bent the knee. {{red-eagle|Faolan}} would not yield, and the elders, afraid of what he would bring on them, cast him out and accepted the Empress's rule.

In exile he gathered the {{reachmen}} who still kept the old ways and struck at the foreigners from the hills. His raids hurt them, but the Empress's soldiers kept coming, and the rebels could not drive them out. Then a {{hagraven}} came to him. She offered him the power to free his people, and the price was his heart. He paid it, and his eyes turned black as opals. After that he fought with a sword that burned with fire, and no one could stand against his rebels. Within two years they had all but driven the foreigners out of {{the-reach}}.

{{hestra}}'s generals came back with a greater army and laid siege to his stronghold for two weeks. At last {{red-eagle|Red Eagle}} came out alone, with no armour, and met them with his burning sword. He killed a thousand men and broke their attack, but that night he was dying. His people carried him into a tomb cut in the rock. He gave them his sword and swore that when {{the-reach}} was free and the blade was returned to him, he would rise and lead them again. Then the tomb was sealed.

## Notes

- **His death.** The Imperial chronicles of {{hestra}}'s reign tell of a rebel leader of {{the-reach}} who was cornered and killed at the cost of a full legion. A song of {{the-reach}} has him fight on with a hundred arrows in him, and an old verse has his bearers carry him up the crag already dead.
- **The oath.** Only one telling has the promise to rise again. The translator of the old verse thinks later seers of {{the-reach}} may have added it.
- **The heart.** In the song, the {{hagraven|hagravens}} plant a briar seed where his heart had been, and a scholar who studied an old cave painting in {{the-reach}} thought he may have been the first {{briarheart}}. In the old verse, the {{hagraven|hag}} takes what she was owed from his breast only at his burial.
- **The tomb.** His tomb in the mountains of {{the-reach}} is known as {{rebels-cairn}}. In the {{era-fourth}} the {{forsworn}} took {{red-eagle|Red Eagle}} as their hero and sought a rite to raise him.
