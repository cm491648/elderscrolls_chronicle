Before there was a world, there were two forces: {{anu}}, which was stillness, and {{padomay}}, which was change. Where they met, spirits arose. These spirits had no past and no future until {{auriel}} took shape. Time came with him, and the others began to know themselves and take names.

One of them was {{lorkhan}}. He went from spirit to spirit with a plan for a new world, {{mundus}}, where everything would have limits and an end. Many agreed. {{magnus}} drew up its design, and {{auriel}} joined once he was promised its rule. Those who gave part of their own being to the work became the {{aedra}}. Others refused. They kept their power whole, made realms of their own in {{oblivion}}, and became the {{daedra}}.

The work cost its makers dearly. As the world took form, the spirits within it grew weak, and some died. {{magnus}} abandoned the work; those who stayed met at the {{adamantine-tower}} to decide what would become of the world, and of {{lorkhan}}, whom they blamed.

## Notes

- **Gift or trick.** Men tend to see {{lorkhan}}'s plan as a mercy that let lesser beings grow; most elves see it as a deception that cut them off from the divine. The {{nords}} honor him as {{shor}}.
- **Whose telling.** The outline follows the creation myths gathered in *The Monomyth*.
- **Names.** Men call this god of time {{akatosh}}.
