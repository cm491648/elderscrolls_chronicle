{{magnus}} drew the plans for {{mundus}}. When {{lorkhan}} persuaded the spirits to make a new world, it was {{magnus}} who worked out how it would be built, and he drew up its designs.

The work had a price no one had foreseen. To give the new world substance, the spirits who built it had to pour in their own power. As {{mundus}} took shape they grew weaker, and some of them faded out of being altogether.

{{magnus}} decided to end the project. In the last birth-pains of the world, he left it and fled back to {{aetherius}}, the realm of the spirits. Where he passed, he tore a hole, and through it the light of {{aetherius}} poured in. That hole became the sun.

Many spirits followed him. They came to be called the {{magna-ge}}, and each of them tore a smaller hole as it went. These became the stars.

The spirits who stayed behind were left to finish the world without its architect. Through the sun and the stars, the light of {{aetherius}} fell on the young world; those who later learned to draw on it called it magic.

## Notes

- **Other accounts.** Some say the stars are not holes but pieces of {{magnus}} himself. The {{bosmer}} say the spirits pierced the wall of night trying to escape the world.
- **Worship.** The {{altmer}} and the {{bretons}} honor {{magnus}} as a god. The {{imperials}} do not count him among the {{eight-divines}}: he gave much, they say, but not all, and they think his gift of magic has done the world as much harm as good.
- **Star spirits.** One of the {{magna-ge}}, Merid-Nunda, later fell in with the {{daedra}} and became the Daedric Prince {{meridia}}.
