Early in the {{era-merethic}}, the {{aldmer}} sailed to {{tamriel}} from their homeland, {{aldmeris}}.

Nine ships made the first landfall, on the northern shore of an island in the {{summerset-isles}}. The first to step onto the beach was the high lord {{torinaan}}. He claimed the island for his people, named it {{auridon}}, and swore a blood-oath never to leave it.

The island was not empty. As the newcomers explored it, they met fierce native beasts. Some of them argued for sailing on to a safer land. {{torinaan}} refused. He stayed, fought the creatures and destroyed them. A short way south of the landing place the settlers built their first city, {{firsthold}}.

From {{auridon}} the {{aldmer}} spread over the rest of the islands, and then to the mainland. Their first colonies lay far apart on islands along the coast. Later settlements were founded inland, mostly in the fertile lowlands of the southwest and the center. Wherever they went, they pushed the native beastfolk into the jungles, marshes and mountains. In {{summerset-isles|Summerset}} they raised the {{crystal-tower}}.

Over the following ages the {{aldmer}} drifted apart. Those who stayed on the islands became the {{altmer}}. Others, farther away, became the elves of {{valenwood}}, the {{chimer}} and {{dwemer}} of the northeast, and the other elven peoples.

## Notes

- **Whose telling.** The landing at {{auridon}} and the story of {{torinaan}} come from {{altmer}} tradition. *Before the Ages of Man*, a chronicle of the early eras, never names {{auridon}}; it says only that {{aldmer|Aldmeri}} refugees left their doomed continent and settled southwestern {{tamriel}} in the middle of the {{era-merethic}}.
- **The homeland.** *Before the Ages of Man* calls {{aldmeris}} doomed and now lost, and identifies it with {{old-ehlnofey}}. Some sources suggest it was never a real place and should be read as allegory.
- **Dates.** {{altmer}} tradition sets the landing early in the {{era-merethic}}; *Before the Ages of Man* puts the settlement of the southwest in the middle of the era.
