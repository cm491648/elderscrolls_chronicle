In 433 of the {{era-third}}, the chapel of {{dibella}} in {{anvil}} was attacked, and the worshippers inside were killed. Outside its doors an old man known only as {{the-prophet|the Prophet}} began to preach. He spoke of {{umaril}}, the {{ayleid}} sorcerer-king whom {{pelinal-whitestrake}} had cut down in the {{white-gold-tower}} early in the {{era-first}}. {{umaril|Umaril}} had bound himself to the {{daedra|Daedric}} Prince {{meridia}}, so his death in the tower had not finished him. Now, {{the-prophet|the Prophet}} said, he had come back to take revenge on the gods, and the killings in {{anvil}} were his work.

Arms alone would not stop him. {{umaril|Umaril}} had to be beaten twice, once in the flesh and once in the spirit, and that called for the favor of the gods and the {{crusaders-relics}}, the armor and weapons the {{eight-divines}} had given {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}}. When {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}} fell, the elves had cut his body into eight pieces, and the relics were scattered. One of those who heard the sermon set out on a pilgrimage to the wayshrines of the gods across {{cyrodiil}}. At its end the pilgrim saw a vision in which {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}} spoke. In an {{ayleid}} ruin the pilgrim then found the remains of {{amiel-lannus}}, the old order's founder, with the crusader's helm, and his journal led the way to the {{priory-of-the-nine}}.

The priory had been the seat of the {{knights-of-the-nine}}, an order founded in 111 to recover the relics and broken apart within twenty years. In its undercroft the pilgrim took up the crusader's cuirass, then went after the remaining relics one by one, following the trails of the old knights who had sought them. New knights came to the priory to join, and the order rose again with the pilgrim at its head. Meanwhile {{umaril|Umaril}}'s servants struck a second time and defiled the chapel of {{mara}} in {{bravil}}.

With every relic gathered, {{the-prophet|the Prophet}} named one thing still lacking: the blessing of {{talos}}. {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}} had never held it and never could have, for in his day {{talos}} was not yet a god. Once the blessing was won, the new crusader and the knights marched on {{garlas-malatar}}, an {{ayleid}} fortress where {{umaril|Umaril}} waited with his host. Facing his old enemy's heir, {{umaril|Umaril}} granted, without warmth, that {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}}'s reach was long.

In the depths of the fortress the crusader struck down {{umaril|Umaril}}'s body. His spirit fled, and the crusader followed it into the spirit realm and fought him there. This time {{umaril|Umaril}} did not come back.

## Notes

- **The first fight.** *The Song of Pelinal* has {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}} kill {{umaril|Umaril}} and then fall to the other elf kings; a later volume makes {{umaril|Umaril}} the one who dealt his death. In *The Adabal-a*, {{pelinal-whitestrake|Pelinal}}'s severed head tells his demigod companion that {{umaril|Umaril}} had deceived him and was not truly dead.
- **The old order.** The first knights won back four of the relics. When war broke out over the throne in 121, most of them left, some taking relics with them, and an Imperial decree disbanded the order in 131. Sir {{amiel-lannus}}, its founder, went in search of the helm in 153 and did not return.
