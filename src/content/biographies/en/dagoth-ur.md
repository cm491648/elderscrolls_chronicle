{{dagoth-ur}} was a {{chimer}} lord of the {{era-first}}, head of House Dagoth, one of the Great Houses of {{resdayn}}. He was a powerful sorcerer and enchanter of {{nerevar}}'s generation. Both {{nerevar}} and the {{dwemer}} counted him a friend.

That trust gave weight to the news he brought: proof that the {{dwemer|Dwemer}} high engineer {{kagrenac}} had found the {{heart-of-lorkhan}} beneath {{red-mountain}} and was making a new god from it. In the {{battle-of-red-mountain}} that followed, {{dagoth-ur}} went with {{nerevar}} into the chamber of the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and saw {{kagrenac}} turn his tools on it and vanish. {{dagoth-ur}} took up {{kagrenacs-tools}} and urged that they be destroyed at once. {{nerevar}}, badly wounded, wanted his counselors' advice first, and left the tools in his keeping.

When {{nerevar}} came back with {{almalexia}}, {{sotha-sil}} and {{vivec}}, {{dagoth-ur}} would not give the tools up. They were too dangerous, he said, and only he could be trusted with them. To the others he no longer seemed in his right mind. They took the tools by force; {{dagoth-ur}} and his retainers vanished, and he was taken for dead. Some years later the three counselors used the tools to become the living gods of the {{tribunal}}.

Nothing more was heard of him until 882 of the {{era-second}}. That year the {{tribunal}} went into {{red-mountain}} to renew their power at the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and found {{dagoth-ur}} and his kin waiting. The ambush drove them off, and from then on the three gods could no longer draw power from the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}}. {{tiber-septim}}, whose armies were closing in, was wary of the power in the mountain too, and {{morrowind}} joined his Empire under the {{armistice}}.

For centuries the {{tribunal}} led campaigns into the mountain. They killed {{dagoth-ur}} and his kin many times, but the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} always brought them back. At last the three raised a magical barrier around {{red-mountain}}, kept standing by their worshippers' faith, to hold him in. Ash storms still blew out of the crater and carried sickness with them. In 417 of the {{era-third}} he ambushed the {{tribunal}} again, and his kin took two of {{kagrenacs-tools}}. From then on the three gods grew weaker.

A cult spread through {{vvardenfell}}. Townsfolk dreamed strange dreams, and some of the dreamers attacked their neighbors. Few linked any of this to the enemy in the mountain until an Imperial patrol found one of the cult's hidden shrines. Its lone survivor came back swollen and raving with a disease that twisted the flesh, carrying the words of the shrine's priest: the Sixth House had risen, and {{dagoth-ur}} was its lord. House Dagoth, its followers said, had only slept, and had woken to drive out foreign rulers and false gods.

In 427 of the {{era-third}}, as the {{ashlanders}} began to hail a stranger as the {{nerevarine}}, {{dagoth-ur}} called to that stranger as {{nerevar}}, through his priests, a letter and dreams of a figure in a golden mask. They had been friends and brothers, he wrote, until {{nerevar}} struck him down while he kept his oath; still, he would forgive his old lord if the hero came to {{red-mountain}}. Only when {{vivec}} took the hero as his champion did he tell what the {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} knew: {{dagoth-ur}} had never left the chamber of the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}}, and there he was building a giant mechanical god, a second {{numidium}}. The hero went down into the mountain and met him in its halls. He offered no bargain, and said he meant to share the power of the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} with his followers, drive the Empire from {{morrowind}} and cast down the {{tribunal-temple|Temple}}'s gods. In the chamber beyond, beside the giant, the hero broke the enchantments on the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}}. {{dagoth-ur}} died, and the giant was destroyed.

{{vivec}} described him as a mad god, by turns lucid and deranged, wise and bestial. The {{ashlanders}} called him the {{sharmat}}. In {{vivec}}'s sermons the {{sharmat}} is the false dreamer, who waits within {{red-mountain}}. By 432 of the {{era-third}}, Imperial accounts listed him among those destroyed in the fury of the {{nerevarine}}.

## Notes

- **Whose account.** The years up to his disappearance follow {{vivec}}'s private record, as on the battle's page. The Dissident Priests' summary *Kagrenac's Tools* agrees that he was mortally wounded and driven off.
- **His own words.** He said that {{nerevar}} struck him down as he guarded the treasure he had sworn to defend, and that he had served his lord faithfully and been repaid with death.
- **The murder.** In the tradition of {{nerevar}}'s shield-companion Alandro Sul, {{dagoth-ur}} killed {{kagrenac}} himself. The three counselors poisoned {{nerevar}}, and when {{dagoth-ur}} tried to avenge him he was driven off and thought dead.
- **Temple doctrine.** The {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} books *Saint Nerevar* and *Lives of the Saints* call House Dagoth traitors who stood with the {{dwemer}}; *Saint Nerevar* says {{nerevar}} was mortally wounded fighting {{dagoth-ur}}.
- **An anonymous history.** *The Real Nerevar* says House Dagoth sided with the {{dwemer}} in a religious quarrel, and that both houses were wiped out at {{red-mountain}}.
- **His name.** He signed his letter to the hero "Lord Voryn Dagoth, {{dagoth-ur}}".
- **The waking.** A {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} report written for {{vivec}} holds that he woke with his kin in {{era-second|2E}} 882, bound them to the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and began the giant in secret that same year. The report admits that much of its timeline rests on inference.
- **An open question.** Facing the hero, he said he did not know what had become of the {{dwemer}}, though he had long studied {{kagrenac}}'s work.
