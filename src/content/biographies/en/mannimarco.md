{{mannimarco}} studied magic with the {{psijic-order}} on {{artaeum}} in the same years as {{vanus-galerion}}; both were gifted students. {{mannimarco}} took up necromancy in secret, raising the dead and enslaving souls. {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} found him at his rites and told him his studies must end. {{mannimarco}} refused and asked {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} to join him instead. Brought before the Ritemaster {{iachesis}}, he pleaded for necromancy to be allowed as a field of study. {{iachesis}} refused, stripped him of his robes and sent him from the isle to the mainland. {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} protested that his masters had loosed a wolf on the flock, and soon left {{artaeum}} himself to found the {{mages-guild}}. The two remained enemies from then on.

On the mainland {{mannimarco}} sought out forbidden lore: writings on enthralling the dead, and cursed artifacts that necromancers brought him from across {{tamriel}}. He drew their power into himself until his body was a living corpse whose strength and life kept growing. From renegade mages, outcast witches and {{reachmen}} he built the {{worm-cult}}, a hidden order of necromancers whose cells answered to him alone. His followers called him the {{mannimarco|King of Worms}}. In most lands raising the dead was held to be an atrocity, so the cult worked underground, and wherever he could, {{mannimarco}} persuaded, bribed or frightened the authorities into looking away.

The guild that {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} had founded opposed both his necromancy and his cult. {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}}, honored in the guild as Archmagister Emeritus, spent years rooting out the cult's cells. In a memorandum to the guild's magisters he also warned against soul-trapping. The guild had never made it part of its ordinary teaching, he wrote, because it was a branch of necromancy and invited abuse; outside a few special cases kept under strict control, it was to be shunned. He blamed {{mannimarco}} for spreading it among mages until soul gems were sold in every market, and saw this as a plan to make necromancy seem ordinary and harmless.

In {{cyrodiil}} the throne was held by the {{longhouse-emperors}}, a dynasty from {{the-reach}}. Their High Chancellor, {{abnur-tharn}}, headed the {{elder-council}}. He was a {{nibenay|Nibenese}} noble who served the dynasty grudgingly and thought it unfit to rule, and its last emperor, {{leovic}}, had married his daughter {{clivia-tharn}}. In {{era-second|2E}} 576 {{leovic|Leovic}} made the worship of {{daedra}} lawful, and {{varen-aquilarios}}, Duke of {{chorrol}}, rose against him. {{mannimarco}} counselled {{varen-aquilarios|Varen}} through the revolt, and {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} secretly gave the rebels what he knew of the {{imperial-city}}. In 577 {{varen-aquilarios|Varen}} killed {{leovic|Leovic}} at the foot of the throne and declared himself emperor. As {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}}'s reward he married {{clivia-tharn|Clivia}}, and {{mannimarco}} became his chief adviser.

{{varen-aquilarios|Varen}} believed that only a {{dragonborn}} could rightly hold the throne, and he was not one. {{mannimarco}} offered him a remedy, a changed coronation rite with the lost {{amulet-of-kings}}, and joined the search for it as one of {{varen-aquilarios|Varen}}'s four companions; {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} and {{sai-sahan}} were two of the others. At the rite in the {{imperial-city}} he held the others where they stood, said that he served {{molag-bal}}, and let the glyphs he had written into the ceremony tear open the barrier between the world and {{oblivion}}.

{{varen-aquilarios|Varen}} vanished in the {{soulburst}} and was mourned as dead. {{sai-sahan|Sai Sahan}} and the half-giant warrior of the companions were blamed for the emperor's death and went into hiding with a price on their heads; {{sai-sahan|Sai Sahan}} took the {{amulet-of-kings|amulet}} with him. {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} stayed. {{mannimarco}} was having those who opposed him arrested as enemies of the state, and the executions had begun. To keep his own name off that list, the chancellor pledged loyalty to him and was given charge of the {{imperial-city}}. {{mannimarco}} then persuaded the {{elder-council}} to recognize {{clivia-tharn}} as empress regent. She held the title and he held the power, and her father went on serving him so that she could keep the throne.

{{mannimarco}} had cast the {{mages-guild}} out of the {{imperial-city}} at once, and his {{worm-cult}} took over the guild's seat there, the {{arcane-university}}. The Tharns who now ruled the city declared necromancy a lawful art, and the cult took the guild's place as the throne's sanctioned advisers on magic. {{mannimarco}} kept his allies only while he needed them. He shut {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} away in his castle in {{coldharbour}}, where the cultists ignored the old chancellor's orders, and he turned {{clivia-tharn|Clivia}} against her father with a promise to teach her mastery over life and death.

Cast out of the {{imperial-city}}, the {{mages-guild}} carried on from its halls across {{tamriel}}. When three alliances invaded {{cyrodiil}} in 580 to overthrow the government that answered to {{mannimarco}}, the guild stayed neutral in the {{three-banners-war}}, kept its halls open in every alliance's lands and took members of every race. {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} still held its highest title. Its mages watched the {{worm-cult}}. One report, finished after the journeyman who had gathered it was dead, called the cult a brotherhood of necromancers rather than a mere cult of {{daedra}}, and warned that it meant not merely to rival the guild but to replace it.

When {{molag-bal}} began dragging {{nirn}} into {{coldharbour}}, {{mannimarco}} was his chief servant in the world. He gave captives to the Prince. One of them, whose soul went to {{coldharbour}}, escaped and freed the companions of the rite from their prisons there. He also pressed {{estre}}, who led the {{veiled-heritance}}, into submission to {{molag-bal|Molag Bal}}. Meanwhile he hunted the {{amulet-of-kings|amulet}} that {{sai-sahan|Sai Sahan}} had hidden. In 582 he caught up with the surviving companions at {{sancre-tor}} and told them what he wanted it for: to drain {{molag-bal|Molag Bal}}'s power, trap him inside its great gem and rule in his place as a god. The escaped captive killed him there. His spirit boasted that death was only a step toward immortality, but {{molag-bal}} appeared, said that he had known of the betrayal, and dragged the spirit into {{coldharbour}}. The companions later found him in the Prince's stronghold, chained to a stone table.

By the {{era-third}} he was free again. He kept court in a barrow in the mountains of {{hammerfell}}, and when the {{totem-of-tiber-septim}} came to light he was one of its seven claimants. He wanted the {{mantella}} that drove {{numidium}}, and he said he would take the {{totem-of-tiber-septim|Totem}} into {{aetherius}} and leave his earthly shell behind. In the {{warp-in-the-west}} of 417 he gained the {{mantella}}'s power. A new moon later appeared in the sky, and his followers took it as his divine body.

In 433 he returned to mortal affairs. {{hannibal-traven}} had banned necromancy in the {{mages-guild}}, and many of the mages it drove out came over to the {{worm-cult}}. {{mannimarco}} set them against the guild, meaning to destroy it and take the soul of its {{arch-mage}}. {{hannibal-traven|Traven}} bound his own soul into a great {{black-soul-gem}} to shield its bearer from {{mannimarco}}'s enthralling magic, and the new {{arch-mage}} carried it into {{echo-cave}}. There {{mannimarco}} was slain.

The moon went on rising. Well into the {{era-fourth}}, necromancers brought soul gems to his altars when its light fell, for him to darken so that they could hold the souls of people. A tract of the order, *Necromancer's Moon*, told the faithful that the {{mannimarco|God of Worms}} watched over them and would one day return.

## Notes

- **His people.** In *The Elder Scrolls Online* {{mannimarco}} is an {{altmer|Altmer}}, and the half-giant warrior calls him one in *Chronicles of the Five Companions*. *Arkay the Enemy*, a tract signed only "KW", has its author say he was once a lowly man before he joined the ranks of the gods.
- **His age.** In *Where Were You When the Dragon Broke?*, a speaker who signs as the {{mannimarco|God of Worms}} says he learned his mystery during the {{dragon-break}} of the {{middle-dawn}}. That would place him in the {{era-first}}, long before the student years that the poem *Mannimarco, King of Worms* sets around {{era-second|2E}} 230.
- **The expulsion.** *The Order of the Black Worm*, a {{mages-guild}} report from the {{interregnum}}, says {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} himself had {{mannimarco}} expelled. In *Artaeum Lost* {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} writes that the two took their quarrel to {{iachesis}} together, and that he protested when {{mannimarco}} was only banished.
- **The chancellor's motives.** The half-giant warrior's part of the same chronicle calls {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} an opportunist who sided with {{mannimarco}} at once. Asking for asylum in 582, {{abnur-tharn|Tharn}} said he had given up everything to preserve the empire, and that even a patriot's loyalty had its limits.
- **The founder.** *Origin of the Mages Guild* says {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} left {{tamriel}} for other lands, bitter that the guild had sunk into political infighting. The sources do not say when he left or when he took up its leadership again.
- **The last battle.** The poem *Mannimarco, King of Worms* tells, without a date, of a later war in which {{vanus-galerion|Galerion}} led his mages north to a mountain pass and died there, while {{mannimarco}} seemed destroyed but lived on. The victors took his cursed artifacts back to the guild.
- **The choice.** That captive could either set {{mannimarco}} free or leave him chained. Either way he was at work again by the {{era-third}}.
- **The god and the man.** The {{blades}} concluded that each claimant at the {{warp-in-the-west}} reached its aim in some measure. His followers worshipped the moon as his ascended form, yet he led the cult in person in 433, and the sources do not explain how both were true.
