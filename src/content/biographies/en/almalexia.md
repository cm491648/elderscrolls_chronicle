{{almalexia}} was a {{chimer}} of {{resdayn}} and the queen of {{nerevar}}, the war leader of House {{indoril}}. Little is recorded of her life before the marriage. She remembered that age herself as one of long enmity: for years the {{chimer}} and the {{dwemer}} had been at war, for the {{dwemer}} spurned the {{daedra}} her people worshipped and put their faith in their own metal creations instead. Only the invasion of the {{nords}} brought the two peoples together under {{nerevar}} and the {{dwemer|Dwemer}} king {{dumac}}. When she and {{nerevar}} were married, {{dumac}} gave them a matched pair of swords, the finest work of {{dwemer|Dwemer}} smiths, with blades that burned. {{nerevar}} carried {{trueflame}}. Hers was Hopesfire, and she kept it safe through all the ages that followed.

She was also among her husband's closest counselors, with {{sotha-sil}} and {{vivec}}, then a junior counselor. She never shared {{nerevar}}'s trust in the {{dwemer}}. From the start she and {{sotha-sil}} doubted that a peace between two peoples with such old grievances could last. When {{dagoth-ur}} reported that the {{dwemer}} had laid hands on the {{heart-of-lorkhan}} and meant to make a new god from it, she was among those who urged war, and after {{azura}} confirmed the report the counselors pressed {{nerevar}} again, reproaching him for putting a friendship before the safety of his people. His last attempt to keep the peace failed, and the war she had expected came.

Of her own part in the fighting, only {{vivec}}'s sermons give a picture. In the thirty-sixth sermon, brass war machines of the {{dwemer}} broke down the eleven gates of {{mournhold}}, and {{dwemer|Dwemer}} sorcerers who made war with sound followed them in. {{almalexia}} cast off her cloak and took on a war face of snakes, and those who looked at her were overwhelmed. {{sotha-sil}}, in the same sermon, raised clockwork sea creatures that dragged the enemy machines down under the waves.

When the last battle was fought inside {{red-mountain}}, she waited with the other two counselors on its slopes. {{nerevar}} was carried out to them badly wounded and told them that the {{dwemer}} had vanished but their tools remained. The three advised him to keep {{kagrenacs-tools}} and study them, in case the {{dwemer}} ever came back. He gave way only after she and the others swore by {{azura}}, in words he dictated, never to put the tools to the use the {{dwemer}} had planned for them. {{trueflame}} had been shattered under the mountain, and its pieces were lost. {{nerevar}} died soon afterward, and the tools were given to {{sotha-sil}} to keep.

For some years the oath held. Then {{sotha-sil}}, who had been studying the tools in secret, came to {{almalexia}} and {{vivec}} with a vision of a new world: justice for the nobles, health and plenty for the common folk, and the three of them as its undying guides. Together they went to the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} beneath {{red-mountain}} and used the tools on themselves. Long afterward she would say that it was {{sotha-sil}} who mastered the tools and raised her to her rightful station. {{azura}} appeared, cursed the three for breaking their oath, and promised that {{nerevar}} would return. In that moment every {{chimer}} became a {{dunmer}}, grey-skinned and red-eyed. The three afterward chose their own faces, and {{almalexia}} alone went back to the gold of the {{chimer}}.

With {{vivec}} and {{sotha-sil}} she was worshipped as {{almsivi}}, the three in one, in the faith that became the {{tribunal-temple}}; the "Alm" of that name stood for her. Her people called her the Healing Mother, the Lady of Mercy and Mother Morrowind. She stood for compassion, forgiveness and protection, and she was the patron of healers and teachers and the guardian of the poor and the weak. Her seat was {{mournhold}}, the holy city in the lands of House {{indoril}}, the most orthodox of the Temple's supporters; {{vivec}} said that the {{indoril}} were the House closest to her in spirit. For ages she walked the streets of her city in person, healing the sick, feeding the poor and handing out clothing herself. She wrote short fables for children, each with its moral, and she kept a personal guard of chosen warriors sworn to her service.

At the end of the {{era-first}}, {{mehrunes-dagon}} fell on {{mournhold}} and left it in ruins. {{almalexia}} and {{sotha-sil}} met him there and drove him back into {{oblivion}}. She remembered that day long afterward as one of the many times {{sotha-sil}} had fought at her side. A new {{mournhold}} rose on top of the old city, and she said it had been built by craftsmen working under her guidance.

In {{era-second|2E}} 572 an army from {{kamal}} in {{akavir}} landed in {{skyrim}}, then turned east into {{morrowind}}. The {{dunmer}} gave ground before it until, on a beach west of {{ebonheart}}, the legion under {{almalexia}} stopped and held. {{jorunn}}'s {{nords}}, old enemies of her people, fell on the invaders from behind, a phalanx of {{argonians|Argonian}} warriors came to strengthen her line, and a great wave that {{vivec}} called out of the sea finished the enemy. The {{ebonheart-pact}} grew out of that day, and in later years its council met in her city.

In {{era-second|2E}} 882 the three went to {{red-mountain}} for their yearly renewal at the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and found {{dagoth-ur}}, the lord they had thought dead, waiting for them. He and his kin drove them off, and they could not reach the Heart again. Through the {{era-third}} {{almalexia}} joined the campaigns against the mountain season after season, and her power slowly ebbed. Under the Empire, {{mournhold}} now had a king ruling for the Emperor beside her Temple, and the city lived with two masters who never quite came to blows. {{vivec}} saw that she took her divinity far more seriously than he did, that its loss weighed on her, and that she brooded.

In {{era-third|3E}} 417 she and {{sotha-sil}} were overrun at {{red-mountain}} and lost two of the tools, and {{vivec}} brought them out alive. She went back to {{mournhold}} and kept up her rites, but she no longer showed herself to her people. Her guard, once busy keeping order and protecting the people, now did little but protect her. Her sermons turned to destroying the wicked and punishing the foolish, and those who questioned her teachings sometimes disappeared. The few who still saw her said she seemed less the Lady of Mercy than a warrior queen.

In 427 a stranger whom some called the {{nerevarine}} came to {{mournhold}}. By then {{almalexia}} had begun to come among her people again. To a priestess of her Temple's infirmary, her face shone with hope and power, but her words were dark and bitter. By that year she and {{vivec}} no longer spoke to each other. The stranger took on work for her Temple and, at her request, brought her {{barilzars-mazed-band}}, an old ring kept beneath the temple. Not long afterward, mechanical creatures burst into one of the city's plazas and attacked the people there. Now {{almalexia}} gave the hero tasks of her own. On her orders the hero woke an old {{dwemer}} weather machine in the ruins under the city, and ash storms, never before seen in {{mournhold}}, fell on subjects she thought had doubted her. She then greeted the hero as her {{nerevar}} returned at last, handed over one fragment of {{trueflame}}, and asked for the sword to be found and forged again so that her husband could stand at her side once more. Last, she said that {{sotha-sil}} had gone mad and sent his creatures against her city. He was a maker, she said, not a seer; only she had ever seen what was to come. She sent the hero into his {{clockwork-city}} to end his life.

{{sotha-sil}} was already dead when the hero reached him. {{almalexia}} appeared in the dome where he lay and told what she had done. She had used the ring to come here and kill him, and she had sent his {{fabricants}} against {{mournhold}} herself. The hero was to die in the city as her greatest martyr. She would tell her people that the {{nerevarine}} had died professing faith in her, the one true god, and the prophecy would end there. Then she would rule the {{dunmer}} alone, one god and one faith under her law; the king would bow to her, and any who refused would be destroyed. She called {{vivec}} a poet and a fool. {{sotha-sil}}, she said, had always thought himself better than the other two, and he had died without a word, a silence she took as mockery. She drew Hopesfire, the twin of the {{trueflame}} the hero now carried, but the power she had once drawn from the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} had failed her, and the hero killed her. Hopesfire was taken from her body.

Afterward {{azura}} came to the hero. The death of {{almalexia}}, she said, was a blessing for {{morrowind}} that its people could not yet see, for the Lady of Mercy would in the end have betrayed them as she had betrayed all she loved. The ash storms over {{mournhold}} ended. When {{vivec}} heard the news, he was not surprised; he had feared she might try to harm him too. He called it very sad that it should end this way, after beginning in such glory.

In {{mournhold}} hardly anyone believed the hero. Her priests insisted that no one could kill a living god, and the only rumor in the Temple was that she had left the city for a time and that the Temple meant to keep it quiet. An Imperial guide of {{era-third|3E}} 432 recorded that she and {{sotha-sil}} had been destroyed in the {{nerevarine}}'s fury. After {{red-mountain}} erupted in {{era-fourth|4E}} 5, the {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} passed to priests who restored the worship of {{azura}}, {{boethiah}} and {{mephala}}; {{almalexia}} and her companions were honored from then on only as saints. House {{indoril}}, whose fortunes had been bound to her Temple, lost its political power with it. Nearly two centuries after the eruption, a hidden band of her worshippers on {{solstheim}} was still keeping faith with her, as heretics in the eyes of the new Temple.

## Notes

- **Her birth.** Temple lore places her birth on Mount Assarnibibi, where {{molag-bal}} watched over the ninety-nine lovers of {{boethiah}} who brought her into being.
- **Her forerunner.** In Temple teaching {{boethiah}} was the {{daedra|Daedra}} who anticipated {{almalexia}}, as {{azura}} anticipated {{sotha-sil}} and {{mephala}} anticipated {{vivec}}.
- **The murder.** An {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} tradition holds that the three counselors killed {{nerevar}} while pretending to summon {{azura}} with him, {{almalexia}} with poisoned candles. The {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} denied it.
- **Temple doctrine.** The Temple publicly taught that the three became gods through discipline, virtue and wisdom, and said nothing of the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}}.
- **The siege.** The attack on {{mournhold}} during the war appears only in {{vivec}}'s sermons, which already call the three gods in the days before {{red-mountain}}.
- **The novel.** *2920, The Last Year of the First Era*, a historical novel, has her and {{sotha-sil}} away from the city when {{mehrunes-dagon}} struck, and leaves her gravely wounded after the fight, dreaming that the burning castle was the Imperial Palace.
- **A clockwork sermon.** A sermon of {{sotha-sil}}'s followers says she wept over her burning city, then drove Hopesfire into {{mehrunes-dagon}}'s chest before {{sotha-sil}} finished him.
- **The Ash-King.** *The Arcturian Heresy* says {{almalexia}} summoned {{wulfharth}} to fight beside her against the invaders of 572. *Jorunn the Skald-King* gives the summoning to {{jorunn|Jorunn}} and the {{greybeards}}.
- **When she withdrew.** A healer in the Temple courtyard of {{mournhold}} dates the withdrawal to about the time of the barrier around {{red-mountain}}, ten years before 427; a priestess of the Temple infirmary says the three had not walked among the people for fifty years.
- **The date.** The battle at {{red-mountain}} is placed around {{era-first|1E}} 700, and the rise of the three a few years later.
