{{ysgramor}} was an {{atmorans|Atmoran}} who lived late in the {{era-merethic}}. Nothing is recorded of his parents or of his early years. He had two sons: {{yngol}}, the elder, a careful planner and the finest smith among their people, and {{ylgar}}, the fiercer fighter.

When civil war broke out in {{atmora}}, {{ysgramor}} took his family south across the sea with a fleet of settlers. They came ashore at the northern tip of {{skyrim}}, a land held by the {{falmer}}, and for a time the two peoples lived side by side. More ships arrived, and the settlers built {{saarthal}}, the first city of men in that land. While digging its foundations, they found something deep underground, buried it again and kept it secret. Word reached the elves all the same, and quarrels between the two peoples grew. On the {{night-of-tears}} the elves burned the city, and of all its people only {{ysgramor}} and his two sons reached a ship and escaped. Watching the fire from the sea, he wept. From his tears, which had turned to ebony, {{yngol}} forged a great axe, and {{ysgramor}} named it {{wuuthrad}}, the Storm's Tears.

Back in {{atmora}}, he did not sail for revenge at once. He first worked out a way to write his people's speech in runes, borrowing the principles of elven script, and used it to set down the story of his own life. It was the first history written by men. Only then did he send his sons through the land to gather its best warriors. Five hundred answered, the {{five-hundred-companions}}, and they set out in many ships, each with its own captain and crew. One of the captains was a friend from his youth, to whom {{ysgramor}} himself had given the name Jeek of the River. Jeek's ship, the Jorrvaskr, carried {{ysgramor}}'s closest friends.

The crossing cost him his elder son. A storm scattered the fleet, and {{yngol}}'s ship was lost. {{ysgramor}} went out into the storm to find it, and when it was found, {{yngol}} was dead. His father laid him in a barrow on the coast, later known as {{yngol-barrow}}.

The fleet landed near the ruins of {{saarthal}}, which the {{falmer}} now held, and the warriors attacked and took the city back. Over the field {{ysgramor}} let out a great war cry, and the survivors joined in, cheering the victory and mourning their dead. Then he gave his order: the companies were to go deep into the land and fight the elves without mercy, as the elves would show none to them. The captains, his Circle of Captains, agreed that each crew would go its own way. After a night of feasting, the warriors swore the Oath of the Companions anew: each would stand as shield-brother or shield-sister to any of the {{atmorans|Atmoran}} line whenever their paths crossed again. They kept the name of the {{five-hundred-companions|Five Hundred}}, though many had fallen at {{saarthal}}, in honor of the dead. At dawn the crews left the beached fleet behind.

The first to go was the crew of the Jorrvaskr, twenty-two of the five hundred, under Jeek of the River. They went south, fighting elves on the way, and lost none of their number. Their scout came back from a hill with news of a great stone bird standing alone on a wide plain, its eyes and beak lit with fire. No elf lived anywhere on that plain. The crew's elven captives looked at the statue with fear and told them it was older than the elves themselves. The crew reasoned that whatever the elves feared was worth keeping. They took apart their ship and built a hall from its {{atmorans|Atmoran}} timbers at the foot of the statue, and that valley became their home for the rest of their lives. The statue became known as the Skyforge, and the hall kept the ship's name, Jorrvaskr. For a long time it stood alone. People came to settle around it, and a city grew up on the banks of the White River: {{whiterun}}. Jeek was remembered as its founder. While other crews went on seeking glory in conquest, his was the first to settle down and guard the less warlike people who came after them. The warriors of Jorrvaskr were the beginning of the Companions.

{{ysgramor}} rebuilt {{saarthal}}, and his own crew fought on. After a last battle in a barren mountain pass, they parted from the two crews that had marched with them, never to fight beside them again. Heading east toward the sea, they came upon {{yngol}}'s barrow, and his grief returned. Where a river met the sea, within sight of the hill where his son lay, he ordered a city built so that he could always look upon it from his palace. Elven captives quarried and raised it, and many died at the work. A long bridge was thrown across the river so that no one could pass into the land without his leave, and his palace rose in high spires. This was {{windhelm}}. Beneath the city, a great tomb was made ready for the day he would go to {{sovngarde}}. He ruled as the first king of the {{nords}}.

The war with the elves did not end with him. His crews kept fighting for years, spreading far from the old ruins and pushing the {{falmer}} back one region at a time. One crew was camped near {{red-mountain}}, in the eastern lands, when a young runner from a sister ship reached it with the news that {{ysgramor}} had died. The runner went on to tell the other crews. The captains mourned him, and with his death the time of the Return came to an end. He was not laid in the tomb beneath {{windhelm}}. He had chosen instead to be buried on the northern shore, facing {{atmora}}, the land he still longed for.

His descendants held the throne after him and carried on the fighting for generations. Even {{harald}}, the thirteenth of his line, was still fighting the last of the {{falmer}} in {{skyrim}} before he was crowned High King in 143 of the {{era-first}}. The line ended in 369, when a High King was killed without an heir.

The Companions of {{whiterun}} kept his memory as their own. Several hundred years after his death they had become warriors for hire. They called {{ysgramor}} the first Harbinger and held that no one since had been their true leader; the head of the order took the title of Harbinger only to advise and to settle disputes. Late in the {{era-second}}, when a Harbinger chose a council of trusted warriors, he named it the Circle after {{ysgramor}}'s Circle of Captains. The Companions kept the broken pieces of {{wuuthrad}} at Jorrvaskr. In 201 of the {{era-fourth}}, after the Harbinger Kodlak Whitemane was killed, his funeral fire was lit on the Skyforge, and with that fire the smith of the Skyforge forged the axe whole again. The Companions carried it to {{ysgramor}}'s tomb on the coast northwest of {{winterhold}}. Only when {{wuuthrad}} was set in the hands of the statue of {{ysgramor}} at the door did the wall open. Inside, the Companions freed Kodlak's soul so that it could go to {{sovngarde}}.

By the faith of the {{nords}}, {{ysgramor}} had gone to {{sovngarde}}, to {{shor}}'s great hall, where the honored dead feast. The Harbingers of the Companions hoped to join him there. That same year, when the {{last-dragonborn}} entered {{sovngarde}} to face {{alduin}}, {{ysgramor}} stood at the door of the great hall. He welcomed the newcomer and named the three heroes of old who were waiting to fight.

## Notes

- **One king or many.** The *Pocket Guide to the Empire* suggests that the deeds of {{ysgramor}} may gather together the reigns of several early {{nords|Nord}} kings.
- **Why the elves struck.** This telling follows the inquiry *Night of Tears*, which ties the attack to what lay under {{saarthal}}, found in the {{era-fourth}} to be the {{eye-of-magnus}}. Elven writers instead blame {{ysgramor}} for unspecified "provocations and blasphemies".
- **The storm.** {{nords|Nord}} songs say a goddess of the sky sent the storm that killed {{yngol}}.
- **The elves.** The *Pocket Guide to the Empire* and *Night of Tears* both credit {{ysgramor}} with driving the elves from {{skyrim}}. The *Pocket Guide* itself adds that the elves were not driven from all of present-day {{skyrim}} until {{harald}}'s reign, and gives no year for it.
- **His death.** *Songs of the Return* reports the death only through the runner. It does not say how or where {{ysgramor}} died, although the runner came from a battlefield.
- **Jeek of the River.** *Great Harbingers*, a list of notable Harbingers of the Companions, names Jeek after {{ysgramor}} and calls him the discoverer of the Skyforge, the founder of {{whiterun}} and the keeper of the original oath of the Companions, which has since been lost.
- **The axe.** One of the Companions said in 201 of the {{era-fourth}} that {{wuuthrad}} had been in pieces for a thousand years. No record tells how it was broken.
- **In the great hall.** If the {{last-dragonborn}} had led the Companions, {{ysgramor}} also spoke of Kodlak Whitemane, who had earned his place in {{sovngarde}}.
- **Memory.** Each year in {{windhelm}} the {{nords}} hold a feast for the dead that recalls the attack on {{saarthal}}, and the names of all five hundred are read aloud.
- **Dates.** No year survives for his birth, his reign or his death; all of it falls late in the {{era-merethic}}.
