{{nerevar|Indoril Nerevar}} was a {{chimer}} of House {{indoril}}, one of the Great Houses of the people who had followed the prophet {{veloth}} into the northeast of {{tamriel}}. The {{chimer}} worshipped the {{daedra}}, and {{nerevar}}'s own patron was {{azura}}, whom he consulted on matters of weight. Their kin and neighbors, the {{dwemer}}, scorned the {{daedra}} and the rituals of the {{chimer}}, and trusted in reason and logic instead. The two peoples had long been at war. They also differed in how they were ruled. The {{dwemer}} had a king. The {{chimer}} had no single ruler, only Houses and clans; in time of danger they could name a Hortator, a war leader set over all of them. That was the office {{nerevar}} came to hold.

In the {{era-first}} the {{chimer}} lived under {{nords|Nordic}} rule, their Houses and clans divided and quarrelling among themselves. After 369, while the {{nords}} fought one another over the succession, {{nerevar}} drew the factions together, and the Houses accepted him as their Hortator. A ring helped him hold them. Called Moon-and-Star, it had been forged for him by a {{dwemer|Dwemer}} smith in the service of {{kagrenac}} and blessed by {{azura}}. It made its wearer greatly persuasive and proved who he was, because anyone else who put it on died at once. With it he brought the warring clans under one banner.

The united Houses still could not free the land alone. Only the {{dwemer}}, safe in their underground cities, had never been conquered, and {{nerevar}} sought an alliance with their king, {{dumac}}. In 416 the armies of the two peoples beat the {{nords}} at {{red-mountain}} and drove them out. The land was named {{resdayn}}, and the {{first-council}} gave both peoples a share in its rule, with {{nerevar}} at its head beside the {{dwemer|Dwemer}} king.

Among his own people the pact had doubters. The {{chimer}} and the {{dwemer}} still despised each other, and his closest counselors, his queen {{almalexia}} and his advisers {{sotha-sil}} and {{vivec}}, did not believe the peace could last. {{nerevar}} held the Houses to it for centuries by negotiation and compromise, and by his friendship with {{dumac}}, which had grown into a bond of blood brothers. When he married {{almalexia}}, his friend's wedding gift was a pair of fire-edged {{dwemer|Dwemer}} swords; the one meant for {{nerevar}} was {{trueflame}}. In 461 the two leaders also stood together for {{resdayn}} at the coronation of the emperor Gorieus in {{cyrodiil}}.

The peace broke when {{dagoth-ur}}, a lord trusted by both peoples, reported that {{kagrenac}}, the chief craftsman of the {{dwemer}}, had found the {{heart-of-lorkhan}} beneath {{red-mountain}} and was building a new god with it. To the {{chimer}} this was a mockery of their faith as well as a weapon. {{nerevar}} was troubled. He asked {{dumac}} whether it was true, and {{kagrenac}} angrily asked what right he had to judge the {{dwemer}}. He made a pilgrimage to {{azura}}, who confirmed the report. His counselors chided him for trusting too much in friendship and urged war. He went to {{dumac}} once more, hoping to save the peace, but the two friends quarreled bitterly, and war followed.

{{nerevar}} drew most of the {{dwemer|Dwemer}} armies out of their mountain fortress and pinned them in the field, while he, {{dagoth-ur}} and a few companions entered the chamber of the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} by a secret way. There the {{chimer}} King met the Dwarf King, and both fell, gravely wounded and drained by magic. {{nerevar}} saw {{kagrenac}} turn his tools on the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and vanish with the {{dwemer}} around him. {{dagoth-ur}} wanted the tools destroyed at once. {{nerevar}} had ignored his counselors before and would not decide without them this time. He left the tools in {{dagoth-ur}}'s keeping and had himself carried out to the three. They advised keeping the tools for study. He gave way on one condition: that they all swear by {{azura}} never to use the tools as the {{dwemer}} had intended. Back in the chamber, {{dagoth-ur}} refused to give them up. {{nerevar}} and his guard took them by force, and {{dagoth-ur}} vanished. {{nerevar}} died not long afterward.

For some years the three kept the oath. Then they used {{kagrenacs-tools}} on the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}} and became the living gods of the {{tribunal}}. {{azura}} appeared, cursed them as oathbreakers, and foretold that {{nerevar}}, who had kept his oath, would return to punish them. When {{sotha-sil}} defied her, their skin turned the grey of ash and their eyes turned red. {{azura}} told them that this was their own doing, not hers, and that their people would share their fate to the end of time. At first the three knew only that it had happened to them. Then they saw that it had fallen on every {{chimer}}: the people {{nerevar}} had led were now the {{dunmer}}. The {{dunmer}} afterward called it the Curse, and remembered that they had been {{chimer}} before it.

How {{nerevar}} died depends on who tells it, and the tellings differ widely. {{vivec}}'s own record, given to a dissident priest, does not say; it passes from the oath straight to the years that followed. The {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} pamphlet *Saint Nerevar* says {{dagoth-ur}}, whom it calls a traitor, gave him a mortal wound, and that he lived just long enough to see the Temple born and bless it. The Temple's *Lives of the Saints* calls his death a martyrdom in the moment of victory over the {{dwemer}}. The {{ashlanders}} trace their account to his shield-companion Alandro Sul, and the Temple keeps it among its hidden writings as *Nerevar at Red Mountain*. In that telling the three pretended to call {{azura}} with him and killed him instead, {{almalexia}} with poisoned candles, {{sotha-sil}} with poisoned robes and {{vivec}} with poisoned invocations; {{azura}} then changed the {{chimer}} into {{dunmer}} as a mark of the crime. A collection of {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} legends gathered by Imperial scholars, *Nerevar Moon-and-Star*, says the khans of the Great Houses killed him in secret after the victory. Another Imperial scholar writes in *The War of the First Council* that he was mortally wounded when treason opened the {{dwemer|Dwemer}} fortress, and died of his wounds shortly after. A {{house-telvanni}} retainer's history, *The Real Nerevar*, says simply that he was killed in the battle. The {{nords}} have their own version in the secret song kept with the *Five Songs of King Wulfharth*: {{lorkhan}} himself dealt him a mortal wound, and he feigned death long enough to cut out the {{heart-of-lorkhan|Heart}}. {{vivec}} later told the {{nerevarine}} that the three had not murdered him, and that the {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} story was persuasive in places but false.

The {{tribunal-temple|Temple}} honored him as Saint {{nerevar}} the Captain, patron of warriors and statesmen. The {{ashlanders}} remembered him as {{nerevar|Nerevar Moon-and-Star}}, the Great Ashkhan and Hortator who had sworn on his ring to honor their spirits and their rights to the land, and they waited for him to come back and keep that promise. The ring itself had been lost at {{red-mountain}}. In 427 of the {{era-third}}, a stranger who wore it was accepted as the {{nerevarine}} by four {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} tribes and named Hortator by three of the Great Houses, and went on to destroy {{dagoth-ur}}. That same year {{almalexia}} greeted the hero as her husband returned and had the hero forge {{trueflame}} anew.

## Notes

- **Whose account.** The main story above follows {{vivec}}'s record, *The Battle of Red Mountain*, which is also the main version of the {{battle-of-red-mountain}} event.
- **The vanishing.** Only later was it known that in that same moment the {{dwemer}} had vanished not only from the chamber but from every one of their cities.
- **Titles.** {{vivec}}'s record calls him General {{nerevar}} and, in the chamber, the {{chimer}} King. {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} worshippers call him Great Ashkhan and Hortator, and the Temple calls him First Councilor.
- **The curse.** In {{vivec}}'s record {{azura}} denies that the change was her act; in the {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} account she makes it on purpose. {{vivec}}'s sermons say the change of color was {{boethiah}}'s wish, brought about by {{vivec}}'s grief.
- **The ring.** The {{house-telvanni}} history calls {{nerevar}} a warlord who meant to rule all the clans, and says he had the ring made in secret for that purpose.
- **An Imperial history.** *The War of the First Council* makes the war a split inside the {{first-council}}: the {{dwemer}} and {{dagoth-ur}}'s house, with {{nords|Nord}} and {{orcs|Orc}} allies, against the other Houses. After early defeats {{nerevar}} was made general of all the Houses' troops and won {{ashlanders|Ashlander}} help.
- **The sermons.** {{vivec}}'s sermons make {{nerevar}} the champion of a {{tribunal}} already divine, and say he killed {{dumac}} at {{red-mountain}}.
- **The other side.** {{dagoth-ur}} later sent word that {{nerevar}} had struck him down beneath {{red-mountain}} while he guarded what he had sworn to protect, and told the {{nerevarine}} that he had served faithfully and been repaid with death.
- **Dates.** The alliance won its victory in {{era-first|1E}} 416. Most accounts place the final battle around 700, perhaps earlier; the chronicle of the {{era-first}} puts the disappearance of the {{dwemer}} in 668.
