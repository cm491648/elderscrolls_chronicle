STYLE A — ILLUMINATED MANUSCRIPT MINIATURE: a miniature painting in the manner of a 14th–15th century illuminated manuscript, like a plate reproduced in a scholarly edition of a medieval chronicle; it must NOT look like concept art. Fine, even ink outlines; flat opaque gouache-like colour areas with gentle tonal modelling; slightly stylised medieval perspective; small stylised figures with expressive gestures; sky and water as flat lapis-blue fields with small dotted or wavy line patterns; touches of gold leaf only on small details (a rim, a highlight). Background of plain bone-white paper around the miniature's irregular edge — no diapered patterns, no ornamental borders.

PALETTE (strict, few colours): ground of clean bone-white paper (#f5f3ee, #ece8dd), even in tone; line work in iron-gall ink with a cool blue-black cast (#1e1c2c) and a softer grey-violet ink (#575468); main colour lapis-lazuli blue (#24508f); vermilion red (#a1281e) used sparingly as the single warm accent; gold (#ad8730) only as thin lines or small touches. No other saturated hues, no brown sepia cast, no orange glow.

LIGHT: flat and even, as in book illustration; no rim light, no bloom, no lens flare, no decorative glowing magic effects (a glow the DESIGN REFERENCE describes is painted as a small, soft touch of colour).

MINIATURE RULES:
- The miniature sits in the middle of the image with an irregular, softly cut edge and a margin of plain bone-white paper on every side (about 4-6% of the width); calm areas of plain paper around the subject.
- Keep the picture light overall: at least a third of the image is untouched or lightly toned paper; no large solid dark masses. Night, storms and interiors are suggested with a band of lapis blue or sparse lines, not painted dark.
- Fire is small and painted in vermilion red, never orange or yellow; smoke is light grey, not black.
- Wood, rope and leather are drawn in grey-violet ink tones, not brown.
